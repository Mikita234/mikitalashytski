# Editorial standard

## Position

Mikita Lashytski is a small web-development studio that designs, builds, repairs, and improves websites, online stores, and automations. The brand earns trust through clear scope, practical judgment, real work, and direct access to the person doing the work.

## Voice

- Calm, specific, competent, compact.
- Human without staged informality.
- Commercial without pressure.
- Technical when the detail helps a decision.
- Confident where evidence exists; precise about estimates and open questions.

## Sentence test

A strong sentence contains at least one of these:

- a concrete deliverable;
- a business outcome;
- a decision criterion;
- a verified fact;
- a useful instruction;
- a clear next action.

Cut sentences that only announce tone, confidence, simplicity, transparency, or modernity.

## Patterns to remove

Treat these as symptoms and rewrite the surrounding thought:

- «где ломается», «что получится», «три нормальных входа», «без давления»;
- «это больше, чем сайт», «не просто X, а Y», «от идеи до запуска»;
- «сайт, который работает», «решение под ключ», «цифровое присутствие»;
- “where it breaks”, “three clear paths”, “without pressure”, “no fluff”;
- “not just X, but Y”, “from idea to launch”, “a website that works”;
- repeated Rescue / Build / DIY labels where ordinary service names are clearer;
- unexplained English words inside Polish, Russian, or Ukrainian prose;
- slash chains, label fragments, fake terminal language, and headings written as interface noise.

Short interface labels may remain stylized when they are part of the visual system. Commercial headings and explanations use normal language.

## Page formulas

### Homepage

Say what the studio builds, for whom, and what the visitor can inspect. Follow with selected work, service formats, process, and contact. Use portfolio evidence before technical stack details.

### Service page

Name the client situation, deliverables, working process, inputs, price range, typical timing, relevant case, and order action. Explain exclusions as scope boundaries in plain terms.

### Case study

Describe context, responsibility, work completed, operational details, verified result, and next improvement. Separate measured facts from estimates.

### Guide or pipeline page

Answer the search question in the opening paragraph. Use question headings when they match real follow-up questions. Give steps, thresholds, examples, and decision rules. End with one relevant service or self-service action.

### CTA

Name the action: “Send project details”, “Request a website audit”, “View the e-commerce case”. Avoid mood-based actions such as “Start the journey”.

## Locale notes

### English

Use short, direct sentences and established product terminology. Remove startup slogans and inflated claims.

### Polish

Use natural Polish service vocabulary. Keep established terms such as SEO, checkout, CRM, Shopify, and WooCommerce. Prefer “strona firmowa”, “wycena”, “zakres”, and “wdrożenie” where they are precise.

### Russian

Use «бриф», «сайт компании», «интернет-магазин», «разработка», «доработка», «аудит», and «заявка». Explain branded terms once. Prefer complete, natural headings over fragments such as «Где ломается».

### Ukrainian

Use «бриф», «сайт компанії», «інтернет-магазин», «розробка», «доопрацювання», «аудит», and «заявка». Build native sentence rhythm and terminology.

## Final review

Read headings alone. They should describe a coherent service story. Then read the page aloud. Remove repeated sentence shapes, unnecessary em dashes, stacked labels, and claims unsupported by the page.
